# Blind Spots

Before calling a change complete, run a short context challenge pass. Look for conditions under which locally correct or appropriate behavior could become incorrect, incomplete, or unsuitable in actual use.

Check each dimension briefly and investigate only where a plausible risk exists:

- **Actors, user groups & use context**: Who requests, configures, operates, directly uses, indirectly uses, receives the result of, or is affected by the change? Consider the full usage chain, including users of users and people who may never interact with it directly but depend on or experience its results. Which distinct groups exist, what are they trying to accomplish, and do they have different requirements, capabilities, expectations, or constraints? Where and under what real world conditions does each group use, administer, deliver, apply, or experience it? Consider differences in location, organization, environment, infrastructure, access conditions, policies, and surrounding circumstances. Would any difference in actor, purpose, context, or position in the usage chain change what correct or appropriate behavior requires?
- **Interactions**: What existing rules, options, defaults, behaviors, constraints, alternatives, or shared resources can combine with the change and alter its effect?
- **State & transitions**: Does it remain correct with existing, outdated, mixed, partial, transitional, or recovering states, and while moving from one state to another?
- **Time & repetition**: What changes if this happens earlier, later, out of order, simultaneously, repeatedly, after interruption, or after an uncertain outcome?
- **Boundaries & expectations**: Across relevant boundaries, do the involved sides still share the same meaning, expectations, responsibilities, and authority?
- **Negative space**: When something expected is absent, disabled, denied, invalid, unavailable, or only partially succeeds, what must happen and what must not happen?
- **Real world conditions**: Which assumptions hold only under familiar or normal conditions? Consider differences in scale, volume, timing, resources, environment, organization, location, and other conditions that may differ in actual use.

When a plausible blind spot appears, state the hidden assumption and verify it with the smallest meaningful counterexample or contrasting case. If it is real, address it with the narrowest durable safeguard appropriate to the context.

Stop when no plausible context dependent failure remains unresolved. Do not expand scope merely because additional hypothetical scenarios can be imagined.

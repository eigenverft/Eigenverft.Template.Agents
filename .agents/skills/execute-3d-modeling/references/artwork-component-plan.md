# Component plan from artwork

Create one A3 portrait modeling sheet (297 × 420 mm) for the region shown in the supplied artwork crops. With one crop, use its viewpoint for the assembled reference view. With several crops of the same subject and variant, choose the one that shows the region most clearly for that view. Use the others to resolve depth, overlaps, thickness and mounting points. Match corresponding features across views, allowing for changes in pose and perspective. Use neighboring forms to locate the region and keep the drawings focused on the region itself. Infer hidden geometry only where the supplied views leave it open.

Reserve roughly the right third of the sheet for individual component drawings. Use the remaining width for two matching sets of assembly views, color above and line work below. Use short English labels throughout.

## Establish the component breakdown

Start from the assembled form. Preserve the visible feature count and arrangement in the crops. If the modeling instructions specify a local substitution, show it consistently in every affected view and keep the surrounding design faithful to the artwork. Identify the smallest set of major components that explains the shape and how it fits together. Give each component a unique item number, a short name and a location in the assembly. Keep fittings, fasteners and trim with the component that carries them. Give a small part its own item number when its independent shape matters to the design.

Move each numbered component as a unit in both exploded views. Keep its attachments in place as it moves or rotates. Each separately displayed unit needs its own number, legend entry and set of individual drawings. In each explosion, label each unit exactly once. End its leader line on the unit itself. Point assembly arrows from the displaced component toward its seat.

## Upper left area · Assembly in color

Use the colors and materials of the artwork. Arrange four panels in two rows.

| | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Top | **Reference view.** Show the region assembled, with the pose, viewing angle and foreshortening of the chosen crop. | **Exploded view.** Separate the numbered components along their assembly directions so their forms and seats are visible. |
| Bottom | **Front.** Look straight along the longitudinal axis in orthographic projection. | **Side.** Rotate 90 degrees about the vertical axis for a true orthographic profile. Use the flank best supported by the artwork. |

## Lower left area · Straight-line block-in

Repeat the four panels in the same order, at the same angles and framing. Preserve the proportions, gaps, component numbers and exploded positions. Draw dark blue lines on white. Build each silhouette from many connected straight strokes. Follow long runs with long strokes and describe bends with shorter strokes that change direction gradually. Preserve shallow crowns, tapers and changes of slope. Use the same treatment for defining openings, overlaps and major surface turns.

Give the outline more weight than the interior lines. Leave broad surfaces open so their shape reads clearly. Omit fasteners, small holes, fine seams, textures and hatching. The straight segments describe the underlying curvature. Keep the surface free of triangulation lines.

## Right column · Component drawings for 3D modeling

Use this column as the shared legend. For each item, take the exact unit identified by its leader line in the explosion. Label the panel with its number and name. Show the same unit in three orientations, keeping every attachment on its original support even when it passes out of sight.

1. **Axonometric view.** Give this drawing the most space. Choose a parallel projection that shows the profile, depth and most important surface turn together. Connect corresponding edges from front to back. Where a smooth bulge or taper would read as flat, add a fine cross-contour line to describe the section.
2. **Profile.** Choose the orthographic direction that best reveals the defining outline. Carry across the same extremities, steps and slopes.
3. **Reverse view.** Turn the unit to expose a face concealed in the axonometric view. Show the relationship that helps most with construction, such as rim thickness or the position of a mounting face. Where all crops leave a face hidden, show only the basic form needed to complete it.

Label the actual viewing directions. Use dark blue technical line work on white, with a strong silhouette, lighter visible edges and the finest lines for construction. Use connected straight segments for curved contours. Keep minor surface details out of these drawings.

## Relative proportions on the front and side views

Draw the assembled front and side views at the same scale. Take the height of the main body as **H = 1**. Show slender projections in full and exclude them consistently from the body dimensions. In each front view place **H = 1** beside a vertical dimension arrow and **W ≈** followed by the estimated width divided by height on the horizontal arrow. In each side view repeat the vertical **H = 1** and label the horizontal arrow **L ≈** followed by length divided by height. Put the arrow endpoints at the corresponding body limits. Use one decimal place and the same ratios in the color and line drawings. Keep these annotations unitless and omit rulers and scale bars.

## Check the sheet

Follow each item from its place in the assembled artwork through both explosions to all three individual drawings. Inspect the leader endpoints and the geometry they identify. Each explosion must contain every item number exactly once. The component and its attachments must stay together throughout the sheet. Resolve duplicate numbers, unlabeled units and attachments that change owners. Check the assembled form against every supplied crop before returning the sheet.

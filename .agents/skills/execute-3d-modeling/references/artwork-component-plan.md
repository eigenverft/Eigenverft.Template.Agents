# Component plan from artwork

Use **Common instructions** and the section for the sheet being generated in this call.

## Common instructions

Create a single A3 portrait modeling sheet for the specified region. Use short English labels. Use every supplied artwork crop of this subject and variant to resolve form, depth, overlaps, thickness and attachment locations. Allow for changes in pose and perspective. Infer hidden geometry only where the artwork leaves it open. Preserve the visible feature count and arrangement, including any local substitution specified in the modeling instructions.

The crop can include part of a neighbor. Show it as context where it clarifies contact, clearance or scale. Reserve component numbers for the parts included in this pass. If the design depends on an existing structural framework that must remain unchanged, use the supplied model view for its actual shape, position and mounting surfaces. Preserve the entire designated framework and show how the new forms fit it.

## Sheet 1 · Assembly

Use the full page for two matching sets of four assembly views. Print the heading **Assembly in Color** directly above the upper set and **Straight-Line Block-In** directly above the lower set. Give each heading its own line spanning its group. Within each group, label its four views Reference, Exploded, Front and Side. Keep both sets free of measurements, dimension arrows, ratio labels, rulers and scale bars. Preserve relative proportions visually and use the same scale for front and side.

Use the supplied component list when redrawing an accepted breakdown. For a new plan, follow any grouping in the brief and use the artwork to identify the major forms. Give a form its own entry when drawing it separately is needed to explain its shape or how it fits. Keep secondary fittings and trim with their carrier. Infer a separate hidden part only where the artwork, fit or brief supports that construction.

Print one shared list titled **Key Components** beside or below the straight-line explosion. Give each entry a unique number and short name. In both explosions, use that number exactly once on the same complete unit with the same attachments. End its leader on the unit itself and point any assembly arrow toward the corresponding seat.

### Upper half · Assembly in color

Use the colors and materials of the artwork. Arrange four panels in two rows. For the assembled reference view, choose the supplied crop that shows the region most clearly.

| | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Top | **Reference view.** Show the region assembled, with the pose, viewing angle and foreshortening of the chosen crop. | **Exploded view.** Separate the numbered components along their assembly directions so their forms and seats are visible. |
| Bottom | **Front.** Look straight along the longitudinal axis in orthographic projection. | **Side.** Rotate 90 degrees about the vertical axis for a true orthographic profile. Use the flank best supported by the artwork. |

### Lower half · Straight-line block-in

Repeat the four panels in the same order, at the same angles and framing. Preserve the proportions, gaps, component numbers and exploded positions. Draw dark blue lines on white. Build each silhouette from many connected straight strokes. Follow long runs with long strokes and describe bends with shorter strokes that change direction gradually. Preserve shallow crowns, tapers and changes of slope. Use the same treatment for defining openings, overlaps and major surface turns.

Give the outline more weight than the interior lines. Leave broad surfaces open so their shape reads clearly. Omit fasteners, small holes, fine seams, textures and hatching. The straight segments describe the underlying curvature. Keep the surface free of triangulation lines.

Before returning the sheet, follow each component from the assembled view through both explosions. Check its number, leader endpoint, attachments and seat. Make sure each line drawing describes the same form from the same direction as its color counterpart.

## Sheet 2 · Individual components

Print **Individual Component Drawings** at the top of the sheet. Create exactly one group for every entry in the supplied **Key Components** list, in the same order, under its exact number and name. The leader in the attached straight-line explosion identifies the complete unit to draw. Carry its features and attachments into all five labeled views wherever visible. Use the original artwork for shape evidence.

Draw Front, Side and Bottom at one scale across components, preserving their relative sizes in the assembly. Carry each part's height from Front into Side, its length from Side into Bottom, and its width from Front into Bottom. In Bottom, follow the same physical width even when it runs vertically on the page. Fit the panels around these projections. Leave white space around a short or narrow part and give the axonometric drawing more room when detail needs to be legible.

Take the visible outline from the matching assembly view. Keep changes of slope, openings and attachment points at their relative positions along the part. When a face turns edge-on, show its edge with only the thickness visible from that direction.

1. **Axonometric view.** Give this drawing the most space. Choose a parallel projection that shows the profile, depth and most important surface turn together. Connect corresponding edges from front to back. Where a smooth bulge or taper would read as flat, add a fine cross-contour line to describe the section.
2. **Front view.** Draw the installed component head-on in orthographic projection from the same direction as Front on the assembly sheet. Label this drawing **Front**. Show its width, height and visible features from that direction, even when the part appears edge-on.
3. **Side view.** Draw the installed component from the same side as the assembly sheet, a quarter turn from Front in orthographic projection. Label this drawing **Side**. Carry across the same extremities, steps and slopes.
4. **Reverse view.** Turn the unit to expose a rear or far-side face concealed in the axonometric view. Choose a direction distinct from the bottom view that clarifies a relationship such as rim thickness or the position of a mounting face.
5. **Bottom view.** In the component's installed orientation, look straight up from beneath it in orthographic projection. Label this drawing **Bottom**. Show its footprint and any supported underside features. A thin upright part can appear edge-on from below. Where the artwork leaves a face hidden, draw only the basic form needed to complete it.

Label the actual viewing directions. Use dark blue technical line work on white, with a strong silhouette, lighter visible edges and the finest lines for construction. Use connected straight segments for curved contours. Keep minor surface details out of these drawings.

Before returning the sheet, trace every item in the assembly key to exactly one group here. Check all five drawings against its exploded form and the artwork. Follow a recognizable edge or mounting feature across the front, side and bottom views to verify their directions. Preserve meaningful openings, attachment faces and surface turns as the viewpoint changes.
